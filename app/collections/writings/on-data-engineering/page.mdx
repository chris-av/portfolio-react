# On Data Engineering

Data engineering is often described as plumbing — unglamorous work that everything else depends on. That framing undersells it, but it's not entirely wrong.

## What the Job Actually Is

At its core, data engineering is about moving data reliably from where it is to where it needs to be, in a shape that's actually useful.

That sounds simple. It rarely is.

### The Real Complexity

The complexity hides in the edges:

- Schema changes upstream that silently break your pipeline
- Duplicates that only appear under certain partition conditions
- Late-arriving data that makes your aggregations wrong in ways that are hard to detect

> A pipeline that works 99% of the time is not a reliable pipeline. The 1% will always hit production at the worst moment.

## Tools Are the Easy Part

Spark, dbt, Airflow, Kafka — these are learnable. What's harder is developing instincts for data quality, lineage, and failure modes. Knowing which questions to ask before building, not after.

The best data engineers I've worked with think like skeptics. They assume the data is wrong until proven otherwise, and they build systems that surface problems loudly rather than silently swallowing them.

---

1. Understand the source before touching it
2. Model failure as a first-class concern
3. Make pipelines observable from day one
